ConsistTalk: Intensity Controllable Temporal Consistent Talking Head Generation with Diffusion Noise Search

ACM MM 2025 Submission #6540

Abstract

The emergence of diffusion-based video generation has recently led to significant advancements in audio-driven portrait image animation. However, due to the insufficient decoupling of motion and identity information, current methods exhibit significant limitations in background stabilization and flicker mitigation. In addition, existing approaches lack joint modeling of audio and portrait motion, and control of motion intensity is either inflexible or results in severe temporal inconsistencies. To address these problems, we propose ConsistTalk, a diffusion-based approach specialized in improving the visual consistency in generating talking heads with controllable intensity. Specifically, we introduce an optical flow-guided temporal module (OFT) that incorporates facial optical flow and a 3D full-attention-based model to enhance temporal consistency. Second, we present an Audio-to-Intensity (A2I) model obtained through multimodal teacher-student knowledge distillation. The A2I model converts audio and facial velocity features into a frame-wise intensity sequence, representing the overall strength of head and expression movements. Finally, we introduce a plug-and-play, training-free noise initialization strategy (IC-Init) that leverages inference-time noise searching based on frequency decomposition, with the stop frequency adaptively parameterized by the acquired intensity sequence. These methods enable ConsistTalk to generate temporal consistent talking head videos with the ability to adjust portrait movement diversity by manipulating the acquired intensity scores. Extensive experiments indicate that ConsistTalk outperforms recent models, demonstrates the ability to maintain background stabilization and mitigate flicker, and provides more lifelike and high-quality results.

II. Comparison With Current Methods

Qualitative Evaluation

We observe that existing diffusion model–based methods exhibit varying degrees of instability, including incorrect shadows, background flickering, and motion blurring. In contrast, our proposed framework incorporates facial optical flows and the A2I model, enhancing the relationship of audio and portrait motion while employing the IC-Init noise search strategy. This enables our method to generate temporal stable videos with vivid expressions and natural movements.


Quantitative Evaluation

Our method demonstrates significant improvements in temporal consistency, background stability, and identity preservation. Furthermore, it outperforms other diffusion model-based approaches in motion diversity evaluation. We notably excel in consistency-related metrics, 38.7% lower than the recent state-of-the-art model Sonic in the Flicker metric. These enhancements stem from our innovative OFT module and IC-Init strategy. In terms of audio-lip synchronization and visual quality, our method performs on par with the previously top-performing techniques.

Ethical Consideration

We should use technology responsibly and be careful about the synthesized content. There is a risk that EasyTalking could be misused. Thus, we will restrict the access to our code and pre-trained models when making the code public, likely by validating the institutional email. Furthermore, we also consider embedding visible or invisible digital watermarks in any generated content.
