ConsistTalk: Intensity Controllable Temporally Consistent Talking Head Generation with Diffusion Noise Search

AAAI 2026 Submission #25845

Abstract

Recent advancements in video diffusion models have significantly enhanced audio-driven portrait animation. However, current methods still suffer from flickering, identity drift, and poor audio-visual synchronization. These issues primarily stem from entangled appearance-motion representations and unstable inference strategies. In this paper, we introduce ConsistTalk, a novel intensity-controllable and temporally consistent talking head generation framework with diffusion noise search inference. First, we propose an optical flow-guided temporal module (OFT) that decouples motion features from static appearance by leveraging facial optical flow, thereby reducing visual flicker and improving temporal consistency. Second, we present an Audio-to-Intensity (A2I) model obtained through multimodal teacher-student knowledge distillation. By transforming audio and facial velocity features into a frame-wise intensity sequence, the A2I model enables joint modeling of audio and visual motion, resulting in more natural dynamics. This further enables fine-grained, frame-wise control of motion dynamics while maintaining tight audio-visual synchronization. Third, we introduce a diffusion noise initialization strategy (IC-Init). By enforcing explicit constraints on background coherence and motion continuity during inference-time noise search, we achieve better identity preservation and refine motion dynamics compared to the current autoregressive strategy. Extensive experiments demonstrate that ConsistTalk significantly outperforms prior methods in reducing flicker, preserving identity, and delivering temporally stable, high-fidelity talking head videos.

Methodology

Optical Flow Guided Temporal Module

In this paper, we adopt a decoupling strategy: identity (ID) features are encoded by ReferenceNet only once, while motion information is dynamically constructed by estimating facial optical flow between adjacent frames. The core idea is to decouple motion dynamics from identity information in the generated frames, thus ensuring that the model relies primarily on the reference image for appearance features and utilizes preceding frames to capture temporal movement features. To enhance temporal consistency, we employ the Full-Attention mechanism with 3D RoPE, which has demonstrated superior performance compared to divided spatiotemporal (2D+1D) attention. Refer to the figure on the right for a visualization of the proposed OFT module. FA and LN represent the full-attention and the layernorm mechanism, respectively.



Motion Intensity Sequence Modeling

Unlike existing methods (Sonic, Hallo, Loopy), a specific intensity value sequence can be obtained, and multimodal information fusion is explicitly performed instead of modal dropout during training in Loopy. As shown in the figure below, we propose an audio-to-intensity (A2I) module based on teacher-student learning. The teacher model leverages multimodal cross attention and a variant of the gated linear unit to capture the relationships between driven audio and the portrait movement-related features. Then, we employ a knowledge distillation approach to train a student model that predicts the intensity sequence solely from audio input. Specifically, we minimize the mean squared error (MSE) loss between the cross-attention logits of the last \(l\) layers of the teacher and student models.




Intensity Guided Diffusion Noise Search

ConsistI2V introduces noise initialization from the low-frequency band of the first frame based on the key observation that the high-frequency component captures the fast-moving parts and fine details in the video, whereas the low-frequency component corresponds to the large-scale layout. Inspired by this, we first conduct frequency decomposition and construct rewards based on the reference image and preceding frames. In addition, the acquired intensity values are employed to adjust stop frequency and reward coefficients adaptively. Under high-intensity conditions, the system increases high-frequency reward coefficients to enhance motion dynamics. Conversely, under low-intensity scenarios, a larger low-frequency reward coefficient is utilized to reinforce background stability and ID preservation.
As shown on the right, the IC-Init algorithm begins by taking the reference latent \(z_{\mathrm{ref}}\) and injecting gaussian noise for \(\tau\) diffusion steps via \(z_{\mathrm{ref},\tau} = \mathrm{add\_noise}(z_{\mathrm{ref}}, \tau)\), which is used in reward computing (Line 1). Next, the initial beam set \(B_0=\{z^s_0\}_{s=1}^B\) of size \(B\) is created (Line 2). The algorithm then iterates for each frame index \(l = 1\) to \(L\), resetting a temporary budget and new beam set \(B_l\) at each step (Line 3 and 4). For every previous-beam latents \(z_{l-1}^s\in B_{l-1}\), it again adds \(\tau\) steps noise to obtain \(z^s_{l-1,\tau} = \mathrm{add\_noise}(z^s_{l-1},\tau)\), then


samples \(K\) candidates \(z^{s,k}_{l, T}\sim\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol 0,\boldsymbol I)\) for \(k=1,\dots,K\); each \(z^{s,k}_{l, T}\) undergoes a short reverse diffusion of \(T'\) steps to produce lookahead latents \(z_{l, T-T'}^{s,k} = \mathrm{de\_noise}(z^{s,k}_{l, T},\epsilon_\theta,T')\), and the pair \((z_{l,T-T'}^{s,k},z^s_{l-1,\tau})\) is added to the budget. After expanding all \(B\times K\) candidates, the algorithm scores each by the reward function \[ R(z_l,z_{\mathrm{ref},\tau},z_{l-1,\tau},y_l) =\lambda_{\mathrm{low}}(y_l)\langle z_l^{\mathrm{low}},z_{\mathrm{ref},\tau}^{\mathrm{low}}\rangle -\lambda_{\mathrm{high}}(y_l)\langle z_l^{\mathrm{high}},z_{l-1,\tau}^{\mathrm{high}}\rangle \] We select the top \(B\) candidate pairs by the reward function \(R(\cdot)\), then fully denoise them to form the new beam set \(B_{l}\). After processing all \(L\) steps, we have beam sets \(B_1,\dots,B_L\). The final trajectory \(\{\boldsymbol{z}'_l\}_{l=1}^L\) is the one maximizing the cumulative reward across all time steps, thus achieving a balance between reference consistency and motion diversity in a beam-search procedure.

II. Comparison With Current Methods

Qualitative Evaluation

1. In-Domain Comparison

We observe that existing diffusion model–based methods exhibit varying degrees of instability, including incorrect shadows, background flickering, and motion blurring. In contrast, our proposed framework incorporates facial optical flows and the A2I model, enhancing the relationship of audio and portrait motion while employing the IC-Init noise search strategy. This enables our method to generate temporal stable videos with vivid expressions and natural movements.

2. Long Video Generation

We have provided a qualitative comparison below, showcasing generated videos of up to 5 minutes in length. We observe that baseline models (Hallov1&2) tend to introduce cumulative errors over long generations, leading to inconsistent backgrounds, jittering, or flickering. In contrast, our method can generate more temporally stable videos. Our improved performance in long-sequence generation primarily stems from the intensity-guided noise initialization (IC-Init) and the motion feature decomposition, which explicitly separates head dynamics from facial expression flow. Unlike existing approaches that suffer from temporal shifting or background flicker, our method ensures high-frequency expression fidelity without sacrificing background stability.

1. Analysis of the intensity score manipulation

The intensity sequence is obtained from the multimodal teacher-student learning, representing the overall strength of speech prosody and visual dynamics. Then, the intensity value is provided to the denoising UNet model by multiplying it with the hidden states of the audio-conditioned layer. This design allows for explicit control over the motion diversity of the synthesized video by manipulating the intensity scores.
